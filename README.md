# Soko | Product Management Case Study

**Product:** Mobile-First Commerce & Market Sourcing Platform
**Role:** Product Owner / Product Manager (Independent Project)
**Stage:** Functional MVP and early user testing
**Focus:** E-commerce, Marketplace Operations, Product Strategy, UX & Customer Discovery

## 1. Product Overview

Soko is a mobile-first commerce platform designed to help Nigerians living abroad source food and household market products from Nigeria through a more structured and transparent shopping experience.

The product addresses a familiar problem: customers abroad often depend on family members or trusted contacts to identify reliable vendors, assess product quality, negotiate purchases and arrange shipping.

Soko aims to make this experience easier to access, manage and track.

## 2. The Problem

Buying market products remotely presents several challenges:

- Difficulty finding reliable local vendors.
- Limited visibility into product quality and availability.
- Informal ordering and payment arrangements.
- Uncertainty around packing, shipping costs and delivery status.
- Dependence on family members or friends to complete errands.

**Product opportunity:** Create a convenient, trusted shopping experience combining customer-facing commerce with structured operational fulfillment.

## 3. My Role

I led the independent product project, including:

- Defining the product vision and target customer segments.
- Translating user problems into product requirements and workflows.
- Prioritizing MVP features and defining acceptance criteria.
- Designing customer ordering and internal operations journeys.
- Establishing preliminary pricing and fulfillment rules.
- Testing customer account isolation, checkout and order workflows.
- Gathering feedback from early users.
- Making product strategy and launch-sequencing decisions.

## 4. The MVP

### Customer Experience

- Product browsing, search and category filters.
- Product details, quantities and shopping basket.
- Requests for unavailable products.
- Quote review and acceptance.
- Checkout and delivery information.
- Order tracking and account management.

### Operations Dashboard

- Review and manage customer requests.
- Prepare quotes and record purchases.
- Document quality checks with photos.
- Record packed shipment weights.
- Calculate shipping charges using editable rates.
- Update order and fulfillment status.

## 5. Key Product Decisions

### Mobile-First Website Before Native App

I initially prioritized mobile shopping because the target audience was expected to rely heavily on smartphones.

Rather than immediately launching a native mobile app, I decided to build and test a mobile-first website.

**Rationale:** Validate customer demand, improve usability and refine operations before investing in native application distribution.

This remains a product hypothesis to be tested through user research.

### Separate Merchandise and Shipping Payments

I designed the checkout workflow to collect merchandise and service fees initially, with shipping costs calculated after packing and weighing.

**Rationale:** The final shipping cost depends on actual shipment weight, which may not be known at the initial checkout.

### Customer Trust and Quality Assurance

I included quality-check photos, purchase records and order status updates in the operations workflow.

**Rationale:** Remote customers need greater visibility into the quality and progress of their orders.

### Manual Fulfillment Before Scaling

I identified manual fulfillment as a practical way to validate sourcing, customer willingness to pay and operational challenges before expanding automation.

## 6. Business Model and Fulfillment

Initial product assumptions included:

- A 12% service fee with a minimum fee of ₦8,000.
- A minimum order value of ₦100,000.
- Weekly Friday shipping.
- Shipping charges calculated using packed weight.
- Editable shipping rates and currency-conversion assumptions.

These values were used as initial operating assumptions and remain subject to validation.

## 7. Product Testing

Six early users tested shopping and checkout journeys.

Functional testing covered:

- Separation of customer accounts and order information.
- Prevention of unauthorized cross-account data access.
- Product requests and quote acceptance.
- Basket and checkout workflows.
- Shipping-date communication.
- Operational order tracking.

The purpose was to identify workflow failures and usability friction before broader release.

## 8. Product Success Metrics

Metrics identified for future measurement include:

- Visitor-to-basket conversion.
- Checkout completion rate.
- Quote acceptance rate.
- Repeat purchase rate.
- Average order value.
- Fulfillment turnaround time.
- Gross margin per order.
- Customer satisfaction and delivery reliability.

These are proposed measures rather than reported performance results.

## 9. Market Expansion Opportunity

Beyond the diaspora market, I identified a potential domestic expansion opportunity in cities such as Lagos and Abuja.

The target segment would include time-constrained customers seeking reliable sourcing of fresh, safe and high-quality market products.

This expansion remains a future hypothesis requiring further research and demand validation.

## 10. Lessons Learned

**Validate demand early:** Building features is not a substitute for confirming customer willingness to pay.

**Operations are part of the product:** Customer trust depends not only on the interface but also on sourcing, quality checks, payments and fulfillment.

**Prioritize learning over premature scale:** A mobile-first website and manual fulfillment provide opportunities to validate key assumptions before making larger investments.

**Design for customer confidence:** Clear order status, transparent charges and quality evidence are central to the value proposition.

## 11. Next Steps

- Conduct structured interviews with diaspora customers.
- Test willingness to pay through manual fulfillment.
- Measure checkout conversion and order completion.
- Validate sourcing partners and operational costs.
- Improve the customer and operations experience based on evidence.
